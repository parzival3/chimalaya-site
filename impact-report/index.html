Impact report 2017–2026

Every newborn, a home visit

Since 2017 Chimalaya's home-visit nurses, based at the mothers' group clinic in Bode, Madhyapur Thimi, have visited every newborn they can reach in the community: a first visit in the baby's first weeks, two follow-up visits, and a final visit around six months of age. These are the results, from the programme's own records.

The need

The weeks after birth

Almost every baby in our area is now born in a health facility: 96% in our 2017–2019 records. But mother and baby usually go home within a day or two, and the weeks that follow are when breastfeeding is established, when newborn problems such as jaundice or an infected umbilical cord appear, and when families have the least professional support.

Nine in ten mothers needed hands-on breastfeeding help

At the first visit, 89% of mothers had at least one problem with how the baby was positioned or attached at the breast, and only 46% had started breastfeeding within an hour of birth. After a caesarean section, 52% of babies were given formula before their first breastfeed, compared with 13% after a normal birth.

What we do

Four visits in the first six months

Every newborn in the clinic's area is offered the same sequence of visits. The timings below are what actually happened, measured from the programme's records.

VisitTypical timingWhat happens
1stbaby 11 days old Newborn and mother examination, screening for danger signs, breastfeeding position and attachment, hygiene advice. The family receives a Bag4Life bag.
2nd18 days later Weight check, feeding problems, reinforcing exclusive breastfeeding.
3rd38 days after that Exclusive breastfeeding, illness since the last visit, the mother's nutrition, immunisation and family planning.
6 monthsbaby about 7 months old Growth measurement, introducing complementary foods, invitation to the mother groups.

94% of families received the Bag4Life bag at the first visit.

Reach

1,815 newborns since 2017

On average 189 newborns a year, about 16 a month in recent years, from 42 named localities around the clinic. 46% of them are girls.

Continuity of care

Families stay with the programme

96%received the 2nd visit
90%received the 3rd visit
89%received the 6-month visit

Follow-up has become close to universal: for babies first visited in 2023 to 2025, around 99% of families received every follow-up visit, at the intended ages.

Only babies old enough for a visit are counted. The six-month visit is measured from 2019, when the six-month survey started. Part of the shortfall in earlier years is missing paperwork rather than missed visits: nearly all visits without a date have no reason recorded, and only 2 families are recorded as declining.

Outcomes at six months

59% exclusively breastfed for six months

Since June 2021 the six-month visits have been recorded in a digital survey, covering 965 babies so far. 59% were exclusively breastfed for the full six months, as WHO recommends, with no water, other milk or food. Where other food came earlier, mothers most often said they did not have enough breast milk, which our counselling on technique and milk supply targets.

Healthy growth

At a median age of 7 months, 2% of babies were underweight and 9% were short for their age, against the WHO Child Growth Standards.

Advice that reaches every family

At the six-month visit, 964 of 965 families were counselled on nutrition, complementary food, immunisation and family planning.

Looking ahead

What support makes possible

  • Keep every family in the programme as enrolment grows, so the 99% follow-up achieved since 2023 holds.
  • Start breastfeeding support earlier, before and right after birth: half of babies were not breastfed within the first hour.
  • Carry families on into the mother groups, which run from six months to two years.
  • Digitise all four visits, as the six-month visit already is, so outcomes can be followed for every baby from birth.

About the data

This page uses three sets of records kept by Chimalaya's home-visit team: the home-visit register (2017–2026, one row per baby), the detailed first-visit checklist (2017–2019, 584 babies) and the six-month survey (2021–2026, 965 babies). The records were anonymised before analysis, and typing errors in dates and units were corrected by logged rules; dates that could not be resolved were left out rather than guessed. The figures describe the families in the programme; without a comparison group they show what was achieved, not how much is due to the visits alone. Data as of 14 October 2026.
